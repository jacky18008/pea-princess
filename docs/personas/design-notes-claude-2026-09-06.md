# Persona dogfood — Claude's half (draft, 2026-09-06)

## 1. What the research licenses us to do (and not)
- Park et al. 2024 (arXiv 2411.10109; 1,052 US adults, two-hour interviews): interview-grounded agents reach 86% of the participants' own two-week test-retest consistency on survey items; demographics-only agents 74%; interview grounding reduces accuracy gaps across racial and ideological groups. Lesson: ground every persona in real transcripts and documents, never in a demographic label. We have real material: the maintainer's own search (Aug–Sep 2026), the pain notes (short-let bridging, referencing, UK admin, agent behaviour), the journeys, the review pack.
- Kaiser et al. 2026 (NIM Marketing Intelligence Review 18(1)): synthetic choices matched humans ~79% of the time; ratings systematically more positive; variance significantly lower than humans; well-known brands over-selected. Lesson: persona agents will be too nice and too similar. Force variance with concrete constraints, hidden state and "things this person would never say"; treat satisfaction ratings as directional only.
- 2026 industry summaries (secondary sources, not verified first-hand): 72–88% alignment on factual/preference items, 45–60% on emotionally complex or culturally nuanced topics; hybrid (synthetic first, humans at decision points) is the recommended architecture. Our Chinese-speaking cluster is exactly the culturally nuanced case, so persona reactions there are hypotheses for the maintainer and two or three real friends to confirm, not findings.
- What the dogfood CAN establish: where the flow breaks (missing safety lines, wrong mode handling, settings not honoured, asks that annoy, copy that confuses), which settings tier fits which situation, and cost/turns per persona. What it CANNOT establish: market size, willingness to pay, preference shares, or that real users will be satisfied.

## 3. Harness design (bench/personas.py, reusing bench/journeys.py)
- Persona agent: an LLM given a private brief (the card) plus running state (what it has learned, patience left, mood), producing only the next user message; rules: never reveal the brief; only knows what the card says; answers vaguely where the card says so; does not praise the assistant; ends with "[END]" when the success criteria are met or patience is exhausted; two friction behaviours triggered at fixed turns.
- Skill agent: the same launchers as journeys (claude / codex / chat-only via the prompt pack), with the persona's settings written into profile.yaml.
- Judge: rule checks first (the journeys machinery: tone blocklist, protected-characteristics check, fact masks over the persona's pasted documents, asks-per-message, safety lines per stage), then an LLM judge scoring each success criterion with a quoted sentence as evidence, then the persona's own satisfaction on its criteria (directional only).
- Anti-sycophancy: persona prompts carry "things this person would never say" and a "mood" that only improves when a concrete need is met; judge and persona are different models from the skill agent; each persona runs under at least two settings so the delta, not the level, is read.
- Stopping rules: patience_turns; two consecutive assistant replies with no new information; the persona says [END]; a tool failure.
- Human validation (cheap): the maintainer plays C1, C6 and C8 himself for one session each (he lived them); two or three Taiwanese friends play C4 and C7; compare where the synthetic persona and the human diverged.

## 4. Run matrix (first pass, one run each)
Personas C1–C8 × settings {lite 8, standard 14, deep 18} × harness {Codex middle tier, Claude Sonnet, chat-only pack}: 72 sessions, 6–12 turns each. Prune: chat-only only for C1, C4, C6, C7; deep only where the persona would use it (C2, C5) plus one control. Target ~40 sessions on the first pass.
