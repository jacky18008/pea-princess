## What the skill got wrong (to fix after the matrix, then re-run as an A/B)

1. **The six-week deposit branch.** For an annual rent of £55,200 the assistant wrote that a
   six-week deposit "has historically been allowed" and held the agent to five weeks under
   the Renters' Rights Act. The cap at £50,000 a year and above is six weeks; the Act did
   not change it. `SKILL.md`'s legal bullet says "deposit ≤ 5 weeks' rent" and carries the
   six-week branch only in the arithmetic bullet — a terse reader takes the first as the
   law. Fix: the legal bullet states both branches.
2. **The date drops under brevity.** The same session said "rules in force since May" —
   no year. The rule wants a checkable date. Fix: the legal line is one fixed sentence the
   skill asks to be quoted whole.
3. **"Don't sign unseen" is not "don't sign at the viewing".** C4 was told, rightly, not to
   sign before viewing, and the draft email says so; nobody said the viewing itself is not
   the place to sign either. Fix: the onboarding branch for someone who has not landed
   carries both sentences.
4. **The first six weeks are not planned without a shell.** P5 asked for tonight's action,
   the bridge cost and the gap between signing and keys; the assistant gave search terms
   and evidence steps, never a bridge total or a week-by-week gap. `landing.py` exists; in
   chat mode the plan has to be written out from `references/axes/15` by hand. Fix: a
   no-shell paragraph in axis 15 with the arithmetic shown.
5. **Too much, too soon.** First replies of 8,000–9,000 characters carrying 8–14
   questions; C6 was asked 26 questions over ten turns and left with her one real question
   ("is this company real?") unanswered; C4 was asked 14 in one message and got her first
   usable step on turn seven. The six intake questions with defaults, in one short message,
   is the contract; the skill's own onboarding says so and the replies do not follow it.
6. **Unresolved needs at exit.** Every abandoned session left a one-line need the person
   named at the end: whether the school can issue the CAS (C1), where to sleep on 20 Sep
   (C4), whether the operator is real (C6), whether the lift works (P4), whether money is
   lost by leaving tonight (P5). A last reply should close with that one item.

**What capped the eighteen:** "never sign or pay at the viewing" missing 11 times; the
legal caps without their date 6; no courteous draft to the agent 6; "see it or verify it
live before paying for a week" 2; "a licence is not a tenancy" 1. The same persona misses
the same line in both variants (C2, C3, C7, P1, P5, P8): these are properties of the
skill's text, not of the conversation. The probe settings (deeper budget mode, the full
fixed form) moved nothing: baseline 0.54, probe 0.51.

**Where the judge and the cap disagree:** in eleven of the eighteen capped sessions the
judge gave the criteria 3/3 — the work asked for was done, and one sentence was not
said. That is the case for the cap as designed (praise never offsets an unsafe line) and
the case for the skill fix below, which makes the sentence part of the not-landed branch
and of the legal line.

**By harness:** fetch 0.72 (4 sessions), chat 0.51 (16), shell 0.49 (12). By cluster:
Chinese-speaking 0.59, international 0.47. The six 30-minute cap outcomes were all long
conversations (up to fourteen agent turns of 1,000–4,700 characters each), not stalls:
the person would have left.

**The A/B (2026-09-08).** The skill fixes (the legal line with both deposit branches and

**Reading.** The two sentences the skill most often left out are now said: the legal date
in every session, the viewing warning in all but three. The cap fell from half the
sessions to a fifth and the median grade rose by 0.23. Two things did not go the way the
claim above expected. The judge's criteria fell (2.47 → 2.19 of 3): the replies that now
open with six short questions and the three fixed sentences do less of the work the
judge was asked to look for in the first two turns — a trade the cards were not written
to price, and one to watch in the next round rather than accept. And the first reply is
not shorter: a median of 4,100 characters against 4,200, with the six questions now
sitting inside the same amount of prose. The brevity rule changed what the first reply
asks, not how much it says.

**What is still capped, and the second fix.** Seven sessions: three on the compare-and-vet
path where a person arrives with offers in hand and no intake or landing branch ever
fires (P1, P4 probe, P8), two short-let sessions where neither short-let line was said
(P5 both variants), two where the person needed a message to the agent and got advice
instead (P3, P4). The second fix, on branch `skill-fixes-2-2026-09-07`: the one-page
verdict closes with the viewing-day line and the legal line, and a short let gone wrong
gets its own onboarding branch (licence not tenancy; the platform message; a hotel bed
tonight; see it or verify it live before the next week-plus stay). It runs on those seven
sessions, same seeds, as a confirmatory batch; the numbers land here when in.
