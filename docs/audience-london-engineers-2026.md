## 3. Affordability arithmetic

**The rule:** Rightmove's own renter guide states referencing providers check that *"your monthly gross income should be at least 2.5 times the monthly rent, or your annual gross salary should be at least 30 times the monthly rent"* ([Rightmove reference-check guide](https://www.rightmove.co.uk/guides/renter/preparing-to-rent/reference-check/)). The two forms are arithmetically identical; 30× monthly rent is what agents apply.

| Salary | Net £/mo (2026/27, England, no student loan) ■ | Max rent at 30× | ONS London 1-bed £1,752 | ONS London all types £2,317 | Rightmove London asking £2,716 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| £60,000 | £3,780 | **£2,000** | ✅ pass — 46% of net | ❌ fail | ❌ fail |
| £75,296 (ONS London median) | £4,519 | **£2,510** | ✅ pass — 39% of net | ✅ pass — 51% of net | ❌ fail |
| £80,000 | £4,746 | **£2,667** | ✅ pass — 37% of net | ✅ pass — 49% of net | ❌ fail |
| £110,000 | £6,030 | **£3,667** | ✅ pass — 29% of net | ✅ pass — 38% of net | ✅ pass — 45% of net |

Rent benchmarks (all ◆ ONS PIPR, **July 2026**, released 19 Aug 2026 — extracted from the [monthly price statistics dataset](https://www.ons.gov.uk/economy/inflationandpriceindices/datasets/priceindexofprivaterentsukmonthlypricestatistics), London rows): **1-bed £1,752** (+3.2% y/y) · 2-bed £2,218 · flat/maisonette £2,045 · all types £2,317 (+3.0%). UK 1-bed £1,132.
Comparators: ● Rightmove London average advertised rent £2,716 pcm ([Q4 2025 tracker](https://www.rightmove.co.uk/news/content/uploads/2026/01/Rental-Trends-Tracker-Q4-2025-FINAL.pdf)); ● SpareRoom inner-London **room** £979 pcm, outer London £794 ([Q2 2026 index](https://www.spareroom.co.uk/content/info-landlords/rentalindex/)).

**The finding that matters:** a £60k engineer clears the 30× check for an average London one-bed with £248/mo of headroom — but fails it for the average London rental of any size. A woman software developer on the ONS London female median (£60,264) is in exactly this position: her 30× cap is £2,009, i.e. the average one-bed passes and almost nothing else does. Solo one-bed renting in London is a *band*, not a floor, and the tool's users are sitting inside it.

## Example budget for a solo engineer

Persona anchor: one person, one bedroom, no flatmate, on **£75,296** — the ONS London median for their actual occupation code. Take-home £4,519/mo ■ (2026/27 England rates, no student loan).

| Line | £/month | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| **Rent, one-bed** | **£1,752** (realistic band £1,550–£2,050) | ◆ ONS PIPR London 1-bed, Jul 2026 |
| Council tax, Band B–C, 25% single-person discount | £103–£129 | ▲ London avg Band D £2,068 (2026/27) → Band C £1,838 → −25% = £1,379/yr |
| Energy | £85–£115 ■ | scaled below ◆ [Ofgem cap £1,723/yr typical household from 1 Oct 2026](https://www.ofgem.gov.uk/press-release/energy-price-cap-will-rise-4-october-2026); a solo one-bed is low-usage |

| Broadband | £30–£36 | ● UK average ~£34.50/mo, 2026 |
| TV Licence | £15 | ◆ £180/yr from 1 Apr 2026 |
| Mobile + contents insurance | £25–£35 ■ | market estimate |
| **Housing all-in** | **£2,035–£2,117** | **45–47% of net** |
| TfL Travelcard, zones 1–2 | £171.70 | ◆ TfL adult fares 2026 (weekly cap £44.70, frozen to Mar 2027) |
| **All-in incl. commute** | **£2,207–£2,289** | **49–51% of net** |

Passes the 30× check with room (cap £2,510). **Sensitivity:** on the ONS London *female* median for the same job (£60,264) the same flat is 46% of net and housing all-in is 54–56% — the 30× cap falls to £2,009, so the average one-bed still passes but the search space collapses to roughly one property type. Budget the tool's defaults for the £1,550–£2,050 rent band, not for £2,700 asking rents.

## How to write the persona without stereotyping

- **Design for the situation, not the demographic.** The user is *renting alone* — no second income to pass referencing, no one else at the viewing, no one else home at night. Every requirement above follows from living alone, and every one of them is equally true for a man living alone.
- **Include women engineers in the example personas, with real numbers.** The ONS data supports it specifically: ~18,000 women in this exact London occupation, at a median £60,264 — a materially tighter budget than the headline £75,296. Leaving them out would mis-size the product's default rent band by about £500/month.
- **Cite the safety evidence, don't assume it.** "50% of women feel unsafe walking alone after dark near home" is a published ONS figure and belongs in the rationale. "Women want a concierge" is an invention; don't ship it.
- **Never gate features on gender.** No gender field, no gendered defaults, no different results for a woman's account. Safety, verification and walk-route checks are on for everyone; users adjust weights themselves.
- **Newcomer ≠ nationality.** The Chinese-speaking student angle is about *being new to the market* — no rental history, no UK guarantor, judging a street they've never walked, transacting on a platform where fraud is documented. Write it as "first tenancy in the UK", which is also true of a relocating engineer from anywhere.
