# Students: halls, purpose-built student accommodation, or a private flat?

Numbers from `docs/london-rent-levels-2026.md` (sources dated June–August 2026). Read this when the user is a student or asks about halls.

## Three realistic tiers, all-in per month
| Tier | Rent | Bills | Council tax | All-in |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Room in a shared flat, zone 2–3 | £850–1,050 bills-in (£750–950 bills-out) | £0–90 | £0 if every occupant is a full-time student | **£900–1,100** |

## Five things the headline rent hides
1. **Halls and PBSA studios often beat a private one-bed once bills and council tax are added** (a hall studio at ~£1,400–1,500 all-in versus a private one-bed at £1,750 plus bills). Compare all-in, never headline.
2. **The booking channel matters more than the building**: the same room can be far cheaper on a university's nomination or allocation scheme than booked direct. Ask the university's accommodation office before booking any PBSA direct.
3. **2026/27 is a buyer's market for PBSA**: occupancy is down, incentives and cashback are live. Ask.
4. **Council tax exemption needs every occupant to be a full-time student**; one working flatmate and the household pays. Boroughs bill over ten instalments, so the monthly hit is about 20% above annual ÷ 12.
5. **1 April 2027 is a step up**: council tax, water and the TV licence rise, and the temporary VAT relief on electricity ends. Budget the second year higher.

## How the checks change for a shared room
The building checks (EPC, age, heat network, planning, crime, landlord entity, deposit protection) apply unchanged. Add: who the other occupants are and whether the tenancy is joint (everyone liable for everyone's rent) or individual; whether bills are included and capped; the room's own size (`min_room_area_m2`), window and door; and the licensing status of the house (HMO licence where required: `references/boroughs.yaml`).

## Two markets, two referencing regimes
Purpose-built student blocks and university halls are one market: designed for students, paid termly or by the week, referencing is light (often a guarantor form or an upfront term), and in 2026/27 it is a buyer's market with incentives on the table. Build-to-rent towers and private one-beds are a different market: designed for salaried tenants, referencing runs on income (commonly 2.5–3× the annual rent, 2.66× for the self-employed), and a student is routed to a UK guarantor with 4× the rent in income or savings, or to a guarantor product; personal savings alone are often not accepted for students. A student shopping in the second market is not "too poor", they are in the wrong queue: expect friction, ask for the guarantor-product route on the first message, or step back to the first market where the same money buys more and the queue is built for you.
